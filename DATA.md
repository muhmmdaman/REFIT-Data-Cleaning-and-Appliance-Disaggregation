# Data quality and cleaning — REFIT House 1

This note covers Part 1 of the assignment: inspecting raw REFIT House 1 readings and preparing a one-minute dataset. It describes what I checked, the rules I used, and what is still uncertain. The figures and CSV summaries are generated by `House1_Part1_Cleaning.ipynb`.

## Source and coverage

The notebook explicitly requires both raw House 1 parts and stops with a helpful error if either is missing. I used the two raw House 1 files, `RAW_House1_Part1.csv` and `RAW_House1_Part2.csv`, from the [REFIT Electrical Load Measurements dataset](https://pureportal.strath.ac.uk/en/datasets/refit-electrical-load-measurements/) (University of Strathclyde). Together they contain **8,518,763 rows**, covering **9 October 2013, 14:06:17 to 10 July 2015, 11:56:32** according to the Unix timestamps.

The files contain a whole-home `Aggregate` power channel and nine appliance channels (`Appliance1`–`Appliance9`). Values are in **watts (W)**. Readings are irregularly spaced, with a **7-second median interval** after duplicate timestamps are consolidated. The text `Time` field is compared with Unix-derived time on every 100th row. Differences are reported in seconds without rounding. This checks sampled internal consistency, not timezone correctness or every text timestamp. I use Unix seconds consistently and retain the recorded clock without claiming verified UTC or applying an unsupported global hour shift.

The files overlap in time, which explains part of the duplicate problem. Not all duplicates agree, so simply saying they are identical repeated records would be misleading. Missing data is also a significant issue, especially during long outages.

## Cleaning decisions

**Timestamps and duplicates.** I removed rows with invalid Unix timestamps (none were found), sorted by timestamp using a stable sort, and grouped repeated timestamps. For each channel I kept the **last non-missing value** at that timestamp. This is a consistent, simple rule, but it can combine channel readings from different original rows, and it does not prove that a conflicting value is correct. I kept a count of those conflicts rather than hiding the uncertainty.

**Invalid and unusually high values.** Negative readings and readings above **23,000 W for aggregate power** or **4,000 W for an individual appliance** were marked missing before resampling. These upper limits are screening assumptions; House 1's supply rating and individual appliance ratings are not verified. The 4,000 W screen follows the supplied cleaned REFIT notes. No negative values were found; the 3,335 flagged readings exceeded the selected limits. High but plausible changes within those limits were retained. Zero values were not automatically treated as missing. The supplied README states that raw Part1 uses zero for unavailable sensors, while Part2 uses NaN. Part1 zeros can therefore mean either an off appliance or an unavailable sensor; these cannot reliably be separated. Keeping zeros does not establish complete sensor coverage.

**One-minute resampling.** I calculated the mean of valid readings within each one-minute bin. The resulting value is average power in **W**, not energy in kWh. Minutes without valid readings stay missing rather than being set to zero.

**Short gaps and outages.** I used linear interpolation only for missing runs of **one or two consecutive minutes**, provided there were valid readings on both sides. If a minute had observed channel readings but all were rejected by the value screen, I left it missing and did not interpolate through its missing run. This is an offline cleaning choice; it uses surrounding observations and is not a real-time prediction method. Longer gaps were not filled.

## Limitations

- Conflicting duplicate rows cannot be resolved with certainty using the timestamps alone. The last-non-missing rule is practical, not a guarantee of measurement accuracy.
- Fixed power thresholds can incorrectly flag a rare but valid event. I have reported the number affected rather than treating the thresholds as unquestionable ground truth.
- Interpolated short gaps are estimates; they may miss appliance switching within those minutes.
- Long outages remain missing, so the one-minute grid should not be mistaken for complete observed coverage.
- Timestamp checking against the text field was sampled and reports unrounded differences in seconds; it is not an independent timezone calibration.
- Part1 missingness hidden as zeros remains unresolved, so missing-value percentages understate unavailable sensor coverage in that part.
- This manually cleaned raw House 1 dataset is **only for Part 1**. Parts 2 and 3 use the **official cleaned REFIT dataset**, as required by the assignment.

For exact per-channel counts, see `outputs/house1/data_quality_summary.csv` and `outputs/house1/value_audit.csv` produced by the notebook. To reproduce the numbers, place both raw House 1 CSVs in `raw/` and run the notebook from the beginning.
